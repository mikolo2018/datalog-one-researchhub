Mathoepedia Week 2 Mathematics
Detailed teaching plans and competition practice
Primary 6 • Sessions 5–7 of 16 • 45 minutes per lesson

This pack develops percentage reasoning, algebraic modelling, and numerical fluency through three
guided lessons. Each session includes three independent 75-question MCQ practice assessments (225 items
per session), with answer keys. The practice papers are intended for paced work outside the 45-minute
lesson.



How to use the practice assessments
Each assessment contains 75 multiple-choice questions and a separate answer key. For competition
readiness, use one paper at a time under a 60–75 minute limit, without a calculator unless the competition
permits one. Pupils should show working on paper, mark uncertain items, and review errors after scoring.
Use Assessments 1–3 as progressive rounds: first accuracy, then speed, then mixed-condition endurance.

Suggested score interpretation: 65–75 = competition-ready accuracy; 50–64 = strong, review missed
concepts; 35–49 = developing, reteach identified strands; below 35 = targeted practice before timed
retesting. These are instructional guides, not formal grade boundaries.

Session 5 Percentages Money and Ratio
Week 2 • 45 minutes • Source questions 2 and 17

Learning objectives
    Calculate savings before and after percentage changes
    identify percentage bases
    solve ratio-part and unitary-method problems.

Success criteria
    Names the base quantity before calculating a percentage.
    Separates original and changed values in a before-and-after table.
    Uses ratio parts and the unitary method with the known quantity correctly.

Resources: Play money, a 100-square grid, income cards, recipe cards, ratio bars, mini-whiteboards and
calculators for checking only.

Preparation before pupils arrive
Prepare a large 100-square grid, play-money notes, recipe cards, and a two-column “original / changed”
table. Write the worked example values where pupils can see each percentage base.

Worked examples and teacher key
1. Original income ₦1,000; original spending ₦750; original savings ₦250. New income = ₦1,000 × 1.20 =
    ₦1,200. Spending rises by 10% of its original amount: ₦750 + ₦75 = ₦825. New savings = ₦375, so the
    savings increase is ₦125/₦250 × 100 = 50%.
2. In a 5:2 flour-to-sugar recipe, 35 kg of flour represents 5 parts. One part = 35 ÷ 5 = 7 kg; sugar = 2 × 7 =
    14 kg.
3. Share ₦240 in ratio 3:5: total parts = 8; one part = ₦30; shares = ₦90 and ₦150.

Assessment checks and answers
   Savings increase in the worked example: 50%.
   Sugar for 35 kg flour at 5:2: 14 kg.
   Divide ₦240 in ratio 3:5: ₦90 and ₦150.

Homework
   A pupil saves 20% of ₦1,500. Find the savings. (₦300)
   Rice and beans are in ratio 4:3. How many cups of beans are needed for 20 cups of rice? (15 cups)

Support and extension
Support: Use a 100-square grid and a 100-unit income to label the whole before calculating. Offer ratio bars
with the known quantity labelled explicitly.

Extension: Explain why a 20% rise in income does not necessarily mean a 20% rise in savings. Compare
sequential percentage changes with a single net percentage.

Competition practice administration
After the lesson, administer the three 75-item papers as separate practice rounds. Set 60–75 minutes per
round; use a quiet room and require working on paper. Review incorrect answers by strand before moving
to the next paper. The answer key is placed after each paper.
