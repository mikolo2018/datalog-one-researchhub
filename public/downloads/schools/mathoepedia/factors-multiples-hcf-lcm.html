Teacher’s class guide
Lesson purpose: help pupils distinguish factors from multiples, identify primes and composite numbers,
and select HCF or LCM in number problems. Build from multiplication facts and the previous lesson’s
number sense.

Learning outcomes
   List factor pairs systematically and explain why a list is complete.
   Generate multiples and identify common multiples.
   Distinguish prime, composite, and unit numbers; express a composite number as a product of primes.
   Find HCF and LCM by listing or prime factorisation, then verify the result.
   Choose HCF for equal grouping/sharing and LCM for repeated events or common cycles.
Materials: counters/beans, number cards, mini-whiteboards or paper, multiplication charts, coloured
pencils, board, timer, and question sheets. Draw factor-pair arrays if counters are limited.

   During class: ask pupils to explain whether a situation calls for HCF or LCM before calculation; listen
    for confusing factor with multiple or treating 1 as prime.
   After class: mark Set A as a check for readiness; assign Set B for follow-up and Set C for independent

Parent/caregiver responsibilities
   Support punctuality, breakfast, rest, and required stationery for the early class.
   At home, ask the pupil to explain one factor pair and one real-life HCF/LCM example; use 10–15
    minutes of calm practice.
   Encourage the child to show working and correct errors; do not provide answers or turn the practice
    into a high-pressure test.

Differentiation and follow-up
   Entry: every pupil attempts Set A independently to check basic understanding.
   Intermediate: use Set B for systematic lists, factorisation, and everyday contexts.
   Self-development: Set C develops reasoning with three numbers and multi-step applications.
   Support: counters, arrays, skip-counting, and partially completed factor trees; extension: ask pupils to
    invent a situation whose solution requires HCF or LCM.

Recall and explain basic factor and multiple ideas. Use counters, multiplication facts, and lists.

Section A — Objective questions
1. Which list contains all the factors of 12?
   A. 1, 2, 3, 4 B. 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 C. 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 D. 1, 3, 4, 6

2. Which number is a multiple of 7?
   A. 45 B. 46 C. 49 D. 51

3. Which number is prime?
   A. 15 B. 21 C. 17 D. 27

4. Which number is composite?
   A. 2 B. 21 C. 13 D. 19

5. Which is a factor pair of 24?
   A. 4 and 7 B. 3 and 8 C. 5 and 5 D. 2 and 11

6. What is the smallest prime number?
   A. 2 B. 1 C. 0 D. 3

7. Which list shows the common factors of 12 and 18?
   A. 1, 2 B. 1, 3, 6 C. 1, 2, 3, 6 D. 2, 3, 6

8. What is the HCF of 8 and 12?
   A. 2 B. 4 C. 6 D. 8

9. What is the LCM of 3 and 4?
   A. 7 B. 9 C. 12 D. 24

10. What is the first positive multiple of 9?
   A. 9 B. 0 C. 18 D. 1

11. Which is the prime factorisation of 18?
   A. 2 × 9 B. 3 × 6 C. 2 × 3 × 3 D. 1 × 18

12. Which statement about 1 is correct?
   A. 1 is prime B. 1 is composite C. 1 is neither prime nor composite D. 1 is both prime and composite

13. A multiple of 5 always ends in which digit(s)?
   A. 0 or 5 B. 2 or 4 C. 1 or 9 D. 3 or 6

14. What is the HCF of 10 and 15?
   A. 3 B. 5 C. 10 D. 15

Suggested answers / marking points
1. 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18; factor pairs are (1,18), (2,9), (3,6).

2. 6, 12, 18, 24, 30; common multiples of 4 among these: 12 and 24.

3. 12: 1,2,3,4,6,12; 20: 1,2,4,5,10,20; HCF = 4.

4. Multiples of 4: 4,8,12; of 6: 6,12; first common positive multiple is 12.

5. A factor divides a number exactly (5 is a factor of 20); a multiple is the product of a number and a whole
number (20 is a multiple of 5).

Suggested essay marking: 4 marks per item — 1 for an appropriate method, 2 for accurate working, and 1
for answer/explanation with units where relevant. Total 20 essay marks and 20 objective marks per set.
